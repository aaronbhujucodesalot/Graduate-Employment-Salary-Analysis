# Executive Summary

## Graduate Employment & Salary Analysis

This project analyses graduate outcomes across fields of study using a portfolio-ready sample dataset based on publicly documented New Zealand post-study outcome measures. The analysis focuses on median income, employment rate, further study, jobseeker outcomes, overseas status, and long-term income growth.

## Key Findings

Medicine, engineering, and information technology show the strongest income outcomes in the sample data. These fields also perform well when income is adjusted by employment rate, suggesting they provide both strong earning potential and relatively stable labour-market demand.

Natural and physical sciences show a different pattern. Although income outcomes are moderate, further-study rates are high, meaning many graduates may pursue postgraduate education before entering full-time employment. This is important because short-term employment statistics may understate the long-term value of these fields.

Creative arts has lower median income and employment outcomes in the sample data, suggesting graduates may face more variable career pathways. However, salary should not be interpreted as the only measure of graduate success, as creative fields may include freelance work, portfolio careers, and non-traditional employment patterns.

## Business Recommendations

Education providers should present graduate outcomes using a balanced set of metrics rather than salary alone. A more useful decision framework combines employment rate, income, further study, and long-term growth.

Career advisors should highlight fields with strong opportunity scores, while also explaining that some fields have intentionally high postgraduate pathways.

Students should compare fields using both short-term and long-term outcomes. A field with lower immediate employment may still offer strong long-term prospects if it leads to postgraduate study or specialised careers.

## Limitations

The included CSV is a demonstration dataset for portfolio development. A production-ready version should replace it with official TEC post-study outcomes XLSX data. Graduate outcomes are also influenced by regional labour markets, prior work experience, individual choices, and whether graduates work full-time or part-time.
